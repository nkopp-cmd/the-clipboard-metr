## 0. The brief

METR (Model Evaluation and Threat Research) presents itself as an independent nonprofit that measures whether frontier AI systems can act autonomously — and therefore whether they are too dangerous to scale.

That sentence is true as far as it goes.

The rest of the file is about the *rest*:

- METR was incubated inside Paul Christiano’s Alignment Research Center.
- ARC’s founding board was Christiano plus two Open Philanthropy AI people (Holden Karnofsky, Daniel Dewey).
- In March 2024 METR incorporated in Delaware. Its first directors were Beth Barnes, Emma Abele, and Adam Gleave.
- In May 2024 ARC sent METR **$4,477,169** labeled “Program Spin-Off.” Compilers treat METR as parent and ARC as subordinate.
- The same human (Kyle Scott) is treasurer of both charities in the same year, pay split across the two EINs.
- Reviews of lab models are routinely done under NDA. OpenAI has had to approve METR blog posts about those reviews.
- Responsible Scaling Policies — the “if the model can do X, pause” template now used by multiple labs — were prototyped at METR / ARC Evals.
- Philanthropic money (Tallinn / SFF, Audacious / TED, DAFs, Coefficient’s sibling-lab grants) built the cluster. Labs supply **tokens and access**, not cash.
- In September 2026 Dario Amodei proposed embedding METR-style evaluators inside frontier labs with badges and laptops. Anthropic committed first. Sam Altman said OpenAI would match.
- Jane Street individuals appear on METR’s public donor list. Jane Street the firm is an Anthropic shareholder and a large buyer of AI compute.

**What this is not:** a documented cash-for-halt bribe.  
**What this is:** a private standards body built by one network, funded by that network, reviewing the labs under the labs’ legal wrappers, now being invited to live inside the labs — while the same network’s preferred lab asks the industry to “pace” and treat that body as the verifier.

A halt, if it ever happens, will look like those tripwires firing with this cluster holding the clipboard.
